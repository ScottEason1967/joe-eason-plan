The set-in-stone meals

Friday fish fingers & chips

Every Friday, the night before a game. You asked if it's damaging him. It isn't. The night before a match you actually want carbs in the tank, and that's exactly what chips are, so the timing is good, not bad. Two small tweaks and it's a smart pre-match meal rather than a guilty one: oven-bake rather than deep-fry, and put beans or a bit of veg on the side. Fish fingers add a little protein too. Keep the ritual, just cook it that way.

Saturday night takeaway — keep it

His usual: clean double Quarter Pounder (two patties, bun, no sauce or cheese), water, grilled chicken, half a 12" margherita, plain McFlurry, Maltesers to follow. It looks big, but after a match it's a genuinely smart refuel: a lot of protein, plenty of carbs to top the tank back up, and crucially no calories drunk because he sticks to water. Leave it exactly as it is. It's the reward and it does a job.

He's got a serious recovery setup. The kit isn't the hard part, timing is. Two rules do most of the work: sleep long, and don't cold-plunge straight after strength work.

The one timing rule that matters. Cold water straight after strength training blunts the muscle adaptation you just did the session to get. The research is clear on this. So on Thursday, no ice bath right after the two hours of S&C. Use the massage gun and Normatec instead, and if he wants the plunge for sleep or soreness, leave it several hours or take it after the evening football, not after the lifting. Ice baths earn their place after matches, after hard conditioning, and in-season between games, where fast recovery beats adaptation.

Whoop

The referee for the week. Check recovery each morning. Green, crack on. Yellow, keep the plan but drop optional top-ups. Red, pull the extra bike work, protect sleep, ease the day. Track sleep hours and consistency, not just the score.

Ice bath

10–12 min at roughly 11–15°C. Best after Saturday's match/training and any hard conditioning, and Friday if sore. Not within a few hours of Thursday's S&C.

Normatec (legs, hips, knees)

The workhorse. 20–30 min in the evening, safe any day, doesn't blunt adaptation. Use it every hard day, especially Thursday and Saturday. Hips and groin attachments are handy post-injury.

Massage gun

1–2 min per muscle group, post-session or evening. Quads, glutes, calves, hamstrings. Go easy and avoid hammering directly on the groin tendon, work around it.

Nike Hyperboot

Warm mode before sessions to prep feet and ankles, cool mode after for recovery. Handy on the Thursday double and match day.

Concept2 bike

Zone 2 top-ups midweek if the week can absorb it, and the easy Zone 1 flush on Friday. Low-impact, kind to the groin.

Sleep — the biggest lever he owns

9–10 hours, same times where possible. It does more for recovery, adaptation and injury risk than any gadget in the house. Screens down before bed, dark room, and treat Thursday and Saturday nights as non-negotiable. Whoop will show him the difference within a week.

Groin — keep it bulletproof

Cleared for full loading, on the condition he warms up properly every time. So make that non-negotiable and keep loading the adductors so it doesn't come back.

  • Copenhagen adduction progressions, building the eccentric strength the research links to fewer groin problems. Start short-lever, build to full.
  • Adductor squeeze holds (ball between knees) as a quick daily check and activator. If the squeeze feels sore or weak, that's an early warning, log it.
  • Full warm-up before every session: raise, mobilise hips, activate glutes and adductors, then build into sprint/change-of-direction speed. Never cold into a hard cut.
  • Fowler and Brazilian sessions load fast feet and cutting. Warm those specifically.

The part that keeps the groin healthy and his movement clean. One rule covers most of it: move before, stretch after. Dynamic mobility to warm up, static holds to cool down. Ten focused minutes most days beats an hour once a week.

Timing, because it matters. Keep the long static holds for after training or the evening, when he's warm. Static stretching cold, right before a session, actually takes a little off his power and sprint speed. So before a session he moves, after a session he stretches. Hips and adductors get the attention every time, given the groin.

Daily 10-minute flow — every day

Morning or evening, most days. Groin and hips are the priority. Unrushed, breathe through it.

  • Adductor rockbacks ×10 — on all fours, one leg out to the side, rock the hips back. Straight at the groin.
  • World's greatest stretch ×5 each side — deep lunge, hand down, rotate and reach up.
  • 90/90 hip switches ×10 — sat on the floor, rotate both knees side to side.
  • Half-kneeling hip flexor 30s each — tuck the hips under, don't arch the back.
  • Hamstring 30s each — long leg, hinge from the hip.
  • Glute figure-4 30s each.
  • Calf 30s each.
  • Thoracic rotations ×8 each side — open the upper back.
  • Adductor squeeze 3 × 10s — ball between the knees. Doubles as his daily groin check: sore or weak, log it in Track.

Before every session — dynamic warm-up (~8–10 min)

Never static, never cold. This is also the warm-up his clearance depends on, so it's non-negotiable.

  • 2 min easy bike or jog to raise the temperature.
  • Leg swings ×10 front-to-back, ×10 side-to-side each leg (the side swings open the adductors).
  • Walking lunges with a reach ×8 each.
  • Lateral lunges ×8 each — loads the groin through range, safely.
  • Gate swings (hip open / hip close) ×6 each.
  • High knees + heel flicks ~20 m.
  • 3–4 build-up runs to near sprint, the last one with a change of direction. Especially before the Fowler and Brazilian sessions, which load fast feet and cutting.

After every session / evening — static holds

When the muscles are warm. Hold each 30–45s, breathe, two rounds on anything tight.

  • Hip flexors, adductors, hamstrings, glutes, quads, calves.
  • Then foam roll quads, glutes and calves. Work around the groin tendon, not straight on it.
  • Pair this with the Normatec on hard nights (Thursday, Saturday).

Recovery days (Fri & Sun) — the longer one

15–20 min. The daily flow unhurried, plus the groin insurance.

  • Full daily flow, holds a touch longer.
  • Copenhagen adduction progressions — the exercise the research ties to fewer groin problems. Build from short-lever to full over the weeks.
  • Longer foam roll, unhurried.
The whole point: the daily ten minutes and a proper warm-up every session are what stop the groin coming back. It's not glamorous, but it's the difference between playing all season and losing a month to a re-strain.

The honest read on leaning

He's 18, 6'2", about 86 kg, wanting roughly 84 kg, and he led those box-to-box runs from the front, twice through, coming off injury. That's not someone who needs a diet. 86 to 84 is two kilos, and at his height that will very likely drift off on its own through three weeks of preseason volume. So we don't manufacture a deficit. Fuel him fully, keep protein high, and let training do the leaning. Chasing the scale in a return-to-play block is how you slow healing, flatten his sharpness and invite the groin back. If after two or three weeks he's fuelling well and still wants to nudge lower, trim a little on rest days only. Body fat isn't measured, so judge it by the waist, how he looks and how he's performing, not one number.

Why this is Premier League standard, not just more food

  • Protein periodised across the day, not one big evening hit, to keep building muscle around the training.
  • Carbs matched to load so he's fuelled for the hard days and not overfed on the easy ones.
  • Recovery timed on purpose, including keeping cold water away from strength adaptation.
  • Sleep treated as training, tracked and protected.
  • Groin loaded, not rested, using the exercises shown to cut re-injury.
  • Load managed at the week level, protecting two recovery days and giving Whoop a veto.
